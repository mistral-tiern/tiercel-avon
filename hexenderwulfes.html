Hexenderwulfes

Unfinished Sequel Notes — City-State of Mistral Tiern

Status: IN PROGRESS. This adventure was never completed or run. What follows is your original material reorganized into a readable structure — no invented plot, no assumed resolution. Open questions are called out explicitly at the end rather than filled in.

Premise

A cover-up murder within a werewolf noble house spirals into an uncontrolled lycanthropy outbreak in the mountain settlement the house is supposed to protect. The party’s likely entry point is the missing-persons notice for Caeli Brocken — the one loose end the killers haven’t been able to tie off.

The True Backstory (DM Only)

Laid out in order — nothing below is meant to be dumped on players directly.

  1. Cressa Thale, a midwife and secretly a hexenderwulfe (witch), married Sandulf Brocken, a woodcutter and werewolf. They lived with Cressa’s mother Crescentia Harz, a herbalist and fellow hexenderwulfe, and Cressa and Sandulf’s daughter, Caeli Brocken.
  2. After fifteen years of marriage, Sandulf discovered that his wife and mother-in-law had been hiding their witch identity from him the whole time.
  3. Sandulf got drunk at the Serpent’s Stein, went home, and attacked both women — apparently in wolf form, given the wounds (throats torn out, spines shattered as if something bit or clawed through them).
  4. Crescentia got Caeli out before she was killed, telling her she’d catch up later. She didn’t survive to do so. Caeli’s body has never been found — the working assumption in-world is that she’s dead, but nothing confirms it, and Sandulf’s own account of her fate is a lie (see below).
  5. Sandulf went to Sir Vargus Rubeland — a knight of House Talon, the Marquis’s cousin, and himself a werewolf — and framed his confession as self-defense: he claimed his wife and mother-in-law had been using magic to control him.
  6. Sandulf confessed to killing his family, but lied about Caeli specifically — claiming he killed her in the woods and buried the body. He didn’t. Where she actually is (alive, dead, hiding, taken) is not established in these notes.
  7. Vargus, who hates hexenderwulfe witches personally, agreed to help cover up the murders — the notes don’t specify how, only that he agreed.
  8. The cover-up plan: Vargus began infecting local foresters with lycanthropy, intending for an uncontrolled werewolf to take the blame for the original attack. He bit Ortrun Skovard, a forester employed by Blackjack Bull Shelterwood Sawmill.
  9. On the next full moon, a 14-year-old goat shepherdess was found dead near Tuite Thorpe, throat torn out — Ortrun’s first, uncontrolled kill as a newly-turned werewolf.
  10. Vargus then infected a second forester, Laren Schorl, also of Blackjack Bull.
  11. On the following full moon, a farm near Tuite was attacked — no deaths or bites this time, but a witness, farmer Ama Bodean, saw a huge wolf-man creature eat a 1,600-lb cow whole. Her description: chest “as wide as a horse’s,” body “as long as a leopard,” wide snout, long shaggy red hair, glowing eyes.
  12. Some time later, the logging crew’s overnight campsite was found destroyed — blood and gore, bodies of buckers Adelaide Ryland and Sunatar Tumegar recovered (only their heads, per Gudrun Andel’s account), while Ortrun and his father Atli Skovard are both missing entirely. Whether Ortrun (now presumably far along in an uncontrolled transformation) killed his own crew, or something else entirely happened at that campsite, isn’t resolved in the notes.
  13. Officially, Cressa and Crescentia’s deaths are recorded as “accidental animal fatalities” — a cover story that required someone with real influence (Vargus, or House Talon itself) to get past the Royal Watch’s usual investigation.

What this means for House Talon. Vargus isn’t the head of the house — he lost his own bid to become regent for the underage Marquis (see House Talon Structure, below) two years before this story starts. Whether his cover-up is personal freelancing or has any backing from the Marquessa or Marquis is an open question — see Open Questions, below.

The Deeper Lore: Werewolves, Witches, and Vampires

Available via History checks — good material for slow-reveal exposition as the party digs into House Talon’s background.

  • Origins. Born werewolves trace their ancestry to the Harz Mountains, north of Mistral Tiern. The first werewolves were created as soldiers and guardians in service to witches. Werewolf family names include Brocken, Harz, Thale, Rubeland, Quedlin, Schierke, Waldborg, Wernigerode, and Blanken — note that both Sandulf’s and Cressa’s families carry names from this list, which is itself a clue that this was a mixed witch/werewolf household from the start, not a coincidence.
  • The falling-out. During a long stretch of peace, witches began treating werewolves as second-class — worse, in places. Generations of this drove the two groups apart; werewolves formed their own covens, called packs, largely without witches (called Tovenares in this lore).
  • The war. When witches came under attack again, they tried to press werewolf packs back into service. This triggered open war between witches and werewolves. Separately, the witch covens had already gone to war with a vampire clutch that rebelled against the witches’ political dominance over mages and casters. Facing a forced draft, the werewolf packs allied with the vampire clutch against the witches. The werewolves won, hunting the nine witch covens nearly to extinction, and settled into an uneasy truce with the vampires that persists into the present day.
  • Relevance to the main module. This is the same vampire/werewolf/witch history underlying Belladonna (the vampire NPC on Caro’s street) and the werewolf lore already folded into the main booklet — this sequel is where that context pays off directly.

Location: Tuite Thorpe & the Forest Mountain

A small mountain settlement, population 64 — 16 spread-out farms, 5 cottages, a small church/schoolhouse (two clergy), a guard tower, the sawmill, the sawmill house, a smithy, and a tavern. Isolated, prone to being snowed in, generally short on supplies — merchants restocking after winter are a welcome sight.

Location: Temple of the True Spark

Deity: Lady Ashava, the True Spark — Chaotic Good, domains Chaos/Darkness/Good/Repose, symbol a wreath of moss. White timber-framed temple with whitewashed stone, doors always open, lanterns always lit. Interior statue: a seven-foot woman mid-dance before a battered silver disk (the full moon), crowned with a fresh moss wreath — unmistakably lycanthrope-coded iconography for a temple that also handles death rites (repose).

  • Madelon, Mortician (Human) — caretaker, long black hair, olive skin.
  • Yitlish, Priestess (Shallar — gray-skinned, elven-like, gender indeterminate) — holds the heavy iron key to the barrow cemetery where Cressa’s and Crescentia’s remains were interred.

Location: Blackjack Bull Shelterwood Sawmill

Run by Gudrun Andel (Human) — burly, stocky, gruff-voiced, carries a silvered knife (notable, given the subject matter). The crew: two tree fallers (Atli Skovard and his son Ortrun Skovard), two buckers (Adelaide Ryland, Sunatar Tumegar), two choke setters with oxen, six loggers.

Interview: Gudrun Andel. The felling crew never returned with cut trees. Gudrun took Laren Schorl out to the overnight camp around noon and found it destroyed — blood and gore, only Adelaide’s and Sunatar’s heads recoverable, Ortrun and Atli missing entirely.

NPC: The Out of Time Cafe’s Barista

She goes by Gwyneira Gentry in the main shop catalog, Eiragwyn in the Night Dancers notes, and Siarad Serenlaw (“Enna” to those who know her better) here. Treat this as intentional — Eladrin don’t give out their true names, and someone running a shop called Out of Time, built around a mirror-portal, is exactly the kind of person who’d wear a different name for different visitors. This note preserves the “Siarad” persona as written, distinct from her other two:

Description: Exceptionally muscular, northern features, dressed in earthy colors with a green-and-grey handkerchief on her left side — hiding a mass of scar tissue under her clothing. White hair styled after a famous performer; amber eyes that show real wear.

Personality: Clingy toward anyone who shows her appreciation — a trait that held back an earlier writing career. Passes on rewards to people in need and makes a point of leaving places better than she found them.

History: Raised among her parents’ servants before being disowned. Apprenticed in her mother’s forge. Now travels in search of power and loot.

Motivation: To share knowledge with the world, and to grow her business.

5’3” Eladrin. HP 17, AC 18. STR 10, DEX 14, CON 8, INT 12, WIS 15, CHA 13.
